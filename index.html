05 · What the data changed

Four things April got wrong. Stated with the number.

A plan that never gets corrected by evidence was never really measuring anything.

01
The tool alone does not lift conversion. Coaching does.

The May pilot cohort was unambiguous: 35.43% with the Co-Pilot against 26.46% without, a 9-point gap. But across the wider floor at 25% adoption, July reads 19.6% with the tool against 20.8% without. Handle time still improves — 15.0 minutes against 17.8.

This is the most valuable finding of the four months. The lift lives in the coaching wrapped around the tool, not in the tool by itself. That is why the gated coaching sprint moved from a nice-to-have to the centre of the plan — and we found it in 90 days, inside the window where most organisations stall for six to twelve months without ever learning why.

02
The leakage pool relocated. It did not shrink.

April sized the prize at about $86K a month sitting in support tickets that should have converted. Measured honestly, that ticket pool barely moved: general support ran 265 tickets in February — the actual April baseline — and 290 in July. What changed is its conversion, 19.25% to 15.17%, as high-intent traffic moved into the presales lane exactly as designed.

Where the value sits now: the last 7.5 points between the presales lane's 32.45% and the 40% floor PIA has now printed — on a base of 5,134 tickets a month rather than 290. A far bigger prize, and a coaching problem with a candidate exemplar already inside the building.

03
Traffic into the funnel is shrinking across all three brands at once.

Presale ticket-category volume, March to May: ExpressVPN 4,210 → 3,987 → 3,757 · CyberGhost 940 → 934 → 815 · PIA 711 → 598 → 564. Three brands moving together points to one shared upstream cause, not three brand problems.

Candidates we can see: the April eSIM coupon wind-down, and the ExpressAI beta absorbing chats outside the deflection statistics. Chatbot deflection is steady at about 33%, so it is not that. These are clues, not a confirmed cause. A rising conversion rate on a falling base still ends in a smaller number — this is the one item here that could beat us on arithmetic alone, which is why it is in the Ask.

04
Measurement discipline became a deliverable of its own.

April did not anticipate this. Over four months we found the same month reported at four different values depending on how far confirmation had run — not errors, but four points on one curve. So a standard now governs every published number: state the date basis, the confirmation state, the numerator and the denominator, and compare two figures only when all four match.

What it bought us: the $43,592 correction to our own headline, and every published conversion rate recomputing from its own numerator and denominator — 19 of 19 brand-months, 9 of 9 sales-and-ticket statements, 8 of 8 in-text rate triples, all tying, with three May figures deliberately held pending one ruling. When a C-level asks "why should I trust this number", there is a five-minute answer.

The basis behind each of the four findings
Finding 1 — two different instruments. The May cohort figures (35.43% vs 26.46%) come from the pilot conversion report; the July all-agent read (19.6% with vs 20.8% without, 25.0% adoption, handle time 15.0 vs 17.8 min) comes from the Co-Pilot dashboard on a new-customer scope, read Jul 27. They are bridged, never blended, and the May lift is under re-review with a cohort readout due in the first week of August [med].

Finding 2 — which April baseline. The April document's support baseline was February: 51 sales from 265 tickets = 19.25%. July is 44 from 290 = 15.17%. April's own month shows 397 support tickets, but that figure is unruled — a 231-ticket support-to-presales re-split is open, and the report's own basis puts April support at 166. We compare to February because that is what April actually quoted.

Finding 3 — a third denominator. The 4,210 / 940 / 711 series are Zendesk ticket-category counts. They are not sales-opportunity tickets and must never be used as a conversion base. On the sales-ticket denominator ExpressVPN reads 3,708 → 3,767 → 3,668 — essentially flat. That is precisely why this is a watch item and not a declared decline.

Finding 4 — what is still held. Three May figures sit deliberately on their old basis pending the Jul 30 ruling: CyberGhost May tickets and conversion, PIA May tickets and conversion, and ExpressVPN support May. Patching one without the others would leave three consecutive months on three different bases, which our own rule forbids. They are flagged in-report rather than quietly restated.
